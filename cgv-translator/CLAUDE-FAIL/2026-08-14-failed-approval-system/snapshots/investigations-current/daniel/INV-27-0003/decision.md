### Reason

The Hebrew supplies only כְּ + תַּרְשִׁישׁ. The inherited «piedra de Tarsis» added a classifier
noun with no counterpart in the source, and did so in order to prop up a transliteration that
would otherwise be read as the toponym Tarshish.

Retaining the transliteration was considered and rejected: unlike «Mauzim» (Daniel 11:38),
H8658 is a common noun for a stone rather than a name, and «Tarsis» in Spanish points the reader
to a place. The transliteration would therefore create a false referent, which is a larger
departure from the source than choosing among the attested identifications.

«Berilo» is adopted as the identification. Confidence is Medium, not High, because the lexical
tradition is genuinely divided (E-001); the decision resolves a disputed identification and a
future edition may reconsider it.

Scope is Occurrence, not Book Default: H8658 occurs once in Daniel, and this decision does not
purport to govern the breastplate or Ezekiel occurrences in other books.

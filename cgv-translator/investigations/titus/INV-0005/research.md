# Research

## Etymology / sense

οἶκος (house/household) + νέμω (allot / manage) → household manager: one entrusted with administering another’s estate, staff, and resources. Core idea = **entrusted management**, not “oversight” as the episcopal title.

## Option comparison (Spanish)

| Option | Strength | Risk |
|--------|----------|------|
| **mayordomo** | Matches BLE; clear household/parable sense (Luke); still used in Spanish | Can sound archaic or purely domestic in some dialects |
| **administrador** | Clear modern Spanish; fits θεοῦ οἰκονόμον and 1 Cor 4 | Slightly flatter; less “household” color |
| **dispensador** | RV1909; related to “dispensar” resources | In modern Spanish often “one who dispenses/gives out”; can bleed into theological “dispensación” (οἰκονομία) |
| **supervisor** | Appears in some English-gloss Strong’s wording | Collides with ἐπίσκοπος; wrong primary sense for LBF |

## Titus 1:7 discourse

v.7 explains v.5–6: the ἐπίσκοπος must be ἀνέγκλητος **as** θεοῦ οἰκονόμος. The following vices (αὐθάδη, ὀργίλον, πάροινον, πλήκτην, αἰσχροκερδῆ) are failures of a trusted household manager, especially αἰσχροκερδῆ (shameful gain) — fiscal/integrity pressure on a steward.

So the metaphor is stewardship under God, not a second office title.

## Provisional research conclusion (not policy)

Prefer **mayordomo** or **administrador** for LBF; treat **dispensador** as consultative (RV1909); reject **supervisor** for this lemma.

# Developmental Observation Signal Definitions

## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines the developmental observation signals used by the renewed MNA / ROOTS+ direction.

The purpose is not to interpret the text for the student.

The purpose is to make observable developmental behavior visible, markable, teachable, and reviewable.

These signals are not final structure labels.

They are observation tools.

A signal is valid only if it can be directly pointed to in the text.

## 2. Core Rule

Developmental observation must describe textual behavior, not abstract meaning.

Allowed:

- This contrast appears again.
- This pressure has not resolved.
- This earlier burden resumes.
- This exposure formula repeats.
- This development intensifies.
- This closure has not yet occurred.

Not allowed:

- This represents a theological system.
- This symbolizes a doctrine beyond the text.
- This must mean the author is psychologically doing something.
- This theme is important because we already know it from elsewhere.
- This section is about an abstract concept that cannot be repeatedly marked in the text.

## 3. Signal 1 — Recurring Unresolved Contrast

### 3.1 Definition

A recurring unresolved contrast occurs when the text repeatedly places two realities, claims, actions, identities, or outcomes in opposition, and that opposition continues without clear closure.

The contrast may repeat with identical words or with visibly related textual forms.

### 3.2 What Counts as Evidence

Evidence may include:

- repeated opposite terms,
- repeated paired realities,
- repeated negative/positive alternatives,
- recurring claim vs reality collision,
- recurring behavior vs identity collision,
- recurring true/false distinction,
- recurring accepted/rejected distinction,
- recurring old/new or before/after distinction.

### 3.3 What Does Not Count

Do not mark this signal merely because:

- two ideas are different,
- the text changes topic,
- the reader feels tension,
- a theological contrast is known from elsewhere,
- a word appears once,
- a contrast is inferred but not textually repeated.

### 3.4 Observation Questions

Students ask:

- What two things are being placed against each other?
- Does this contrast appear again?
- Has the contrast been resolved, or is it still active?
- Does the same contrast return under different wording?
- Does the text keep using this contrast to expose, test, or develop something?

### 3.5 Visual Exposition

Possible visual display:

- two-color contrast bands,
- recurring contrast markers,
- unresolved span line from first appearance to later appearances,
- repeated contrast tags in the margin,
- closure marker only when the contrast is textually resolved.

### 3.6 Common Drift Danger

Do not turn the contrast into an abstract theme too quickly.

For example, mark light/darkness if the text says light/darkness.

Do not immediately rename it as a theological abstraction unless the text itself repeatedly develops that wording.

## 4. Signal 2 — Repeated Exposure Formula

### 4.1 Definition

A repeated exposure formula occurs when the discourse repeatedly uses recognizable forms to expose a claim, contradiction, false confidence, inconsistency, or hidden reality.

This signal is especially important where the author repeatedly confronts what someone says, assumes, claims, knows, or does.

### 4.2 What Counts as Evidence

Evidence may include repeated forms such as:

- if we say,
- the one who says,
- do you not know,
- if someone says,
- anyone who claims,
- rhetorical questions that expose inconsistency,
- formulaic claims followed by correction,
- repeated denial/correction patterns.

### 4.3 What Does Not Count

Do not mark this signal merely because:

- the author asks one isolated question,
- the reader feels accused,
- the text contains a warning,
- the text states a fact without exposing a claim,
- the formula does not repeat or does not continue a recognizable exposure environment.

### 4.4 Observation Questions

Students ask:

- What claim or assumption is being exposed?
- Is the same kind of exposure repeated?
- Does the exposure formula create continuing pressure?
- Does the formula expose a contradiction between claim and reality?
- Does this exposure environment continue beyond the immediate sentence?

### 4.5 Visual Exposition

Possible visual display:

- exposure icons beside formulas,
- repeated formula highlighting,
- margin labels such as CLAIM / EXPOSURE / CORRECTION,
- span line showing repeated exposure environment,
- grouped display of repeated formulas.

### 4.6 Common Drift Danger

Do not explain the psychology of the exposed person or group.

Only mark the textual exposure formula and what it explicitly exposes.

## 5. Signal 3 — Sustained Testing Pressure

### 5.1 Definition

Sustained testing pressure occurs when the discourse repeatedly evaluates, verifies, tests, or measures a claim, identity, condition, or reality.

The text keeps asking, in effect, whether something is genuine, consistent, valid, or aligned with the stated reality.

### 5.2 What Counts as Evidence

Evidence may include:

- repeated tests of claim vs action,
- repeated tests of identity vs behavior,
- repeated tests of knowledge vs conduct,
- repeated tests of love vs hatred,
- repeated tests of wisdom vs foolishness,
- repeated tests of spiritual claim vs visible reality,
- repeated do/know/say/walk patterns.

### 5.3 What Does Not Count

Do not mark this signal merely because:

- the text gives an instruction,
- the text mentions behavior,
- the reader can turn a statement into an application test,
- the test is imported from theology or tradition,
- the testing pressure is not repeated or sustained.

### 5.4 Observation Questions

Students ask:

- What is being tested?
- What textual evidence shows that it is being tested?
- Does the test repeat?
- Does the same testing pressure continue with different examples?
- Has the testing pressure resolved, or does it keep operating?

### 5.5 Visual Exposition

Possible visual display:

- test markers beside repeated testing statements,
- line connecting claim and tested reality,
- repeated test span across verses,
- paired columns: CLAIM / VISIBLE REALITY,
- unresolved status marker until the test environment closes.

### 5.6 Common Drift Danger

Do not turn every command into a test.

Testing pressure requires repeated evaluative behavior in the discourse, not merely moral instruction.

## 6. Signal 4 — Delayed Closure

### 6.1 Definition

Delayed closure occurs when a development begins but does not resolve immediately. The discourse continues, expands, interrupts, circles, intensifies, or resumes before closure appears.

This signal helps students see long-range continuity.

### 6.2 What Counts as Evidence

Evidence may include:

- a question or tension raised but not answered immediately,
- a contrast introduced and repeatedly revisited,
- a burden stated and then expanded before closing,
- an exhortation that continues through several developments,
- an argument that keeps moving without release,
- repeated return to an earlier pressure,
- closure formula appearing later after continued pressure.

### 6.3 What Does Not Count

Do not mark this signal merely because:

- the passage is long,
- the topic continues generally,
- the student has not understood the point yet,
- there is no clear beginning pressure,
- there is no observable later closure or transition.

### 6.4 Observation Questions

Students ask:

- What began here?
- Has it actually closed?
- What evidence shows that it remains active?
- Does the discourse return to it after interruption?
- Where does the pressure release, resolve, or change direction?

### 6.5 Visual Exposition

Possible visual display:

- open span line from beginning pressure,
- unresolved status marker,
- continuation brackets,
- interruption markers inside the open span,
- closure marker when the text provides resolution or transition.

### 6.6 Common Drift Danger

Do not mark delayed closure simply because a theme can be connected across distance.

There must be observable continuation, return, expansion, or resolution.

## 7. Signal 5 — Re-entry / Restoration

### 7.1 Definition

Re-entry occurs when an earlier developmental pressure resumes after interruption, expansion, illustration, quotation, warning, or side movement.

This signal is essential because a discourse may leave a pressure temporarily without ending it.

### 7.2 What Counts as Evidence

Evidence may include:

- return to an earlier contrast,
- return to an earlier claim/exposure pattern,
- return to an earlier burden statement,
- resumption of a previous exhortation,
- repeated formula after intervening material,
- repeated lexical or conceptual anchor tied directly to earlier development,
- continuation marker pointing back to prior movement.

### 7.3 What Does Not Count

Do not mark re-entry merely because:

- a word repeats accidentally,
- a similar topic appears later,
- the student wants two parts to connect,
- the connection depends on outside theology,
- there is no identifiable earlier developmental pressure.

### 7.4 Observation Questions

Students ask:

- What earlier pressure appears to return?
- What text marks the return?
- What interrupted it?
- Did the interruption end the development, or did the development resume?
- Does the re-entry continue the same unresolved pressure?

### 7.5 Visual Exposition

Possible visual display:

- return arrows,
- re-entry brackets,
- same-color resumed span,
- interruption block inside a larger continuity span,
- margin note: RETURNS TO EARLIER PRESSURE.

### 7.6 Common Drift Danger

Do not mark every repetition as re-entry.

Re-entry requires an earlier active development that is visibly resumed.

## 8. Signal 6 — Continuing Burden

### 8.1 Definition

A continuing burden occurs when the same developmental pressure continues under varied wording, examples, or local movements.

This signal helps observe that a section can remain developmentally unified even when vocabulary, grammar, or examples change.

### 8.2 What Counts as Evidence

Evidence may include:

- repeated appeals toward the same unresolved pressure,
- repeated warnings around the same burden,
- repeated claims developing the same direction,
- different examples serving the same ongoing pressure,
- repeated commands that keep carrying one burden forward,
- recurring assertion with expanded consequences.

### 8.3 What Does Not Count

Do not mark this signal merely because:

- the same topic is broadly present,
- words repeat without developmental movement,
- the reader feels the same idea is present,
- the burden is imported from a sermon outline,
- the wording changes so much that no textual connection can be shown.

### 8.4 Observation Questions

Students ask:

- What burden is being carried forward?
- What repeated textual evidence shows continuation?
- Has the burden shifted or only expanded?
- Are examples serving the same burden or introducing a new one?
- Does the discourse release this burden anywhere?

### 8.5 Visual Exposition

Possible visual display:

- burden span label,
- repeated burden markers,
- same-color directional line,
- nested examples under one burden span,
- continuation marker across changed vocabulary.

### 8.6 Common Drift Danger

Do not name a burden too abstractly.

The burden label must stay close to wording that can be repeatedly shown in the text.

## 9. Signal 7 — Escalation

### 9.1 Definition

Escalation occurs when an active developmental pressure intensifies rather than merely repeats.

The discourse increases weight, urgency, consequence, sharpness, compression, or climactic force.

### 9.2 What Counts as Evidence

Evidence may include:

- stronger wording,
- sharper contrast,
- higher consequence,
- intensified warning,
- expanded consequence,
- climactic summary,
- compressed conclusion after repeated pressure,
- movement from statement to warning to command,
- movement from example to direct address.

### 9.3 What Does Not Count

Do not mark escalation merely because:

- the student feels the passage is emotional,
- the wording is dramatic once,
- a command appears,
- the passage is important theologically,
- the intensity is supplied by the reader rather than shown in the discourse.

### 9.4 Observation Questions

Students ask:

- Is the pressure stronger here than before?
- What changed in the wording or movement?
- Did consequence increase?
- Did the discourse move from statement to warning or command?
- Does this intensification prepare for closure or shift?

### 9.5 Visual Exposition

Possible visual display:

- rising intensity line,
- thicker span line,
- escalation arrows,
- staged markers: statement -> warning -> command,
- intensified color saturation.

### 9.6 Common Drift Danger

Do not mark escalation because of perceived importance.

Escalation must be shown by observable discourse intensification.

## 10. Signal 8 — Resolution / Closure

### 10.1 Definition

Resolution or closure occurs when an active developmental pressure is answered, released, completed, concluded, or clearly transitioned out of.

Closure does not always mean the topic never appears again. It means the active pressure has reached a textual release point.

### 10.2 What Counts as Evidence

Evidence may include:

- explicit conclusion,
- answered question,
- resolved contrast,
- completed argument,
- command following sustained development,
- summary statement,
- result statement,
- therefore/so that style closure,
- transition to a genuinely different pressure.

### 10.3 What Does Not Count

Do not mark closure merely because:

- a chapter ends,
- a paragraph ends,
- a heading appears in an edition,
- the topic seems finished to the reader,
- the text pauses but later resumes the same pressure.

### 10.4 Observation Questions

Students ask:

- What pressure was active?
- What text shows that it has resolved or released?
- Does the pressure return immediately after this?
- Is this closure or only interruption?
- Does a new developmental pressure begin after this point?

### 10.5 Visual Exposition

Possible visual display:

- closure marker,
- span endpoint,
- release symbol,
- resolved contrast tag,
- transition boundary line.

### 10.6 Common Drift Danger

Do not confuse layout boundaries with developmental closure.

Closure must be demonstrated from the discourse itself.

## 11. Signal 9 — Directional Shift

### 11.1 Definition

A directional shift occurs when the discourse begins carrying a different developmental pressure.

This is stronger than a local interruption, illustration, or vocabulary change.

### 11.2 What Counts as Evidence

Evidence may include:

- prior pressure resolves or releases,
- new unresolved pressure appears,
- new repeated contrast begins,
- new exposure environment begins,
- new burden statement appears,
- previous signal pattern drops and another pattern begins,
- new question, command, appeal, or assertion begins carrying the discourse.

### 11.3 What Does Not Count

Do not mark directional shift merely because:

- subject changes,
- verb person changes,
- connector changes,
- topic word changes,
- an illustration begins,
- the reader feels a new paragraph should start.

### 11.4 Observation Questions

Students ask:

- Did the previous pressure close or continue?
- What new pressure begins here?
- What textual evidence shows that it is new?
- Is this a true shift or only an interruption?
- Does the earlier pressure re-enter later?

### 11.5 Visual Exposition

Possible visual display:

- directional boundary line,
- old span ending and new span beginning,
- color shift,
- transition marker,
- side-by-side old pressure / new pressure display.

### 11.6 Common Drift Danger

Do not mark shift by surface change alone.

A directional shift requires change in carried developmental pressure.

## 12. Signal 10 — Sustained Relational Pressure

### 12.1 Definition

Sustained relational pressure occurs when the discourse continues carrying a relational burden across multiple local movements.

This signal is especially common in letters, appeals, corrections, community instructions, and reconciliation texts.

### 12.2 What Counts as Evidence

Evidence may include:

- repeated relational address,
- repeated appeals to receive, love, honor, restore, submit, forgive, remember, or share,
- repeated participation language,
- repeated community identity pressure,
- repeated brother/sister/fellow-worker language,
- repeated relational obligation,
- repeated relational consequence,
- continued pressure toward relational alignment.

### 12.3 What Does Not Count

Do not mark this signal merely because:

- people are mentioned,
- greetings appear,
- the text is an epistle,
- a relationship exists historically,
- the reader feels relational emotion,
- relational pressure is inferred but not carried by repeated textual signals.

### 12.4 Observation Questions

Students ask:

- What relational pressure is being carried?
- What repeated wording or appeal keeps it active?
- Does the pressure continue through other material?
- Does the discourse release the relational pressure?
- Is this relational development central or only incidental?

### 12.5 Visual Exposition

Possible visual display:

- relational pressure span,
- repeated address markers,
- participation markers,
- appeal markers,
- relationship-line overlay.

### 12.6 Common Drift Danger

Do not reconstruct the relationship psychologically.

Only mark the relational pressure actually carried in the text.

## 13. Signal Interaction

Signals often work together.

A large developmental unit may include:

- recurring contrast,
- exposure formulas,
- delayed closure,
- escalation,
- re-entry,
- final resolution.

The goal is not to isolate signals artificially.

The goal is to expose how they combine to make developmental continuity visible.

## 14. Student Training Pattern

For every signal, students should learn to answer four basic questions:

1. What exactly is visible in the text?
2. Does it repeat, resume, intensify, or resolve?
3. What evidence marks its continuation?
4. What evidence marks its closure or shift?

## 15. Evaluation Standard

A developmental observation is valid only if:

- it can be pointed to in the text,
- it can be marked repeatedly or clearly,
- it does not depend on outside theology,
- it does not require abstract speculation,
- it helps observe continuity, development, closure, or shift.

If a signal cannot meet these standards, it should not be used.

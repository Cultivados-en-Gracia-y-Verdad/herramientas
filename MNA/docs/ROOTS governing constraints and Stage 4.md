# ROOTS Governing Constraints and Stage 4 Audit Standard

## Purpose

This document formalizes the governing constraints that control ROOTS analysis and Stage 4 review.

The purpose of these constraints is:

- to preserve objectivity,
- to prevent reviewer drift,
- to maintain reproducibility,
- to protect mechanical consistency,
- and to ensure that ROOTS remains governed by explicit methodology rather than reviewer intuition.

This document is authoritative for:

- Stage 1–4 processing,
- review logic,
- confidence assignment,
- audit procedures,
- and future automation.

------

# 1. ROOTS Governing Principles

## 1.1 Mechanical Before Interpretive

ROOTS is governed mechanically before interpretation is considered.

Structural decisions must be made from:

- Greek morphology,
- syntax,
- clause structure,
- and connector relationships.

Structural decisions may NOT be governed by:

- rhetorical force,
- theological importance,
- perceived centrality,
- persuasive instinct,
- or discourse prominence.

------

## 1.2 Explicit Rules Override Reviewer Intuition

The reviewer submits to the methodology.

The methodology does not submit to the reviewer.

No reviewer may override:

- finite verb rules,
- clause rules,
- dependency rules,
- connector classifications,
- or trunk reduction rules,

based on perceived importance or interpretive preference.

------

## 1.3 Reproducibility Over Elegance

ROOTS prioritizes reproducible output over rhetorically satisfying output.

A mechanically reproducible result is preferred over:

- elegant reduction,
- persuasive rhetorical flow,
- thematic coherence,
- or interpretive smoothness.

If two reviewers following the same rules cannot reliably reproduce the same result, the method is considered unstable.

------

## 1.4 Greek Structure Governs

Greek morphology and syntax govern ROOTS structure.

Not:

- English translation flow,
- readability,
- theological tradition,
- sermon logic,
- or reviewer instinct.

Finite verbs, clause boundaries, and dependency relationships are determined from the Greek text.

------

## 1.5 Constraint Exists to Prevent Drift

ROOTS intentionally constrains:

- intuition,
- interpretive optimization,
- rhetorical weighting,
- theological harmonization,
- and reviewer preference.

This constraint system exists specifically because reviewers naturally drift toward interpretive reasoning.

ROOTS mechanics are intended to resist that drift.

------

## 1.6 Confidence Must Reflect Mechanical Certainty

Confidence levels must reflect:

- confidence within ROOTS mechanics,
- confidence within explicit methodological rules,
- and confidence within structural evidence.

Confidence may NOT reflect:

- rhetorical persuasiveness,
- interpretive satisfaction,
- thematic coherence,
- or reviewer conviction.

A structurally invalid conclusion may not receive elevated confidence regardless of how persuasive it appears.

------

# 2. Connector Classification Authority

## 2.1 Coordinating Connectors

Examples:

- καί
- δέ
- ἀλλά
- οὖν
- μήτε

Effect:

- coordinate clauses,
- do not create removable dependency by themselves.

------

## 2.2 Subordinating Connectors

Examples:

- ἵνα
- ὅτι
- ἐάν
- εἰ
- καθώς
- ὡς

Effect:

- create dependency relationships.

These classifications govern trunk reduction mechanically.

------

# 3. Trunk Reduction Authority

## 3.1 Mechanical Dependency Removal

Subordinate clauses introduced by subordinating connectors are removable during trunk reduction.

This decision is mechanical.

The following categories do NOT override dependency removal:

- perceived importance,
- exhortational force,
- rhetorical prominence,
- thematic centrality,
- theological weight,
- or discourse significance.

------

## 3.2 Reviewer Override Prohibited

Stage 4 review may:

- correct classification mistakes,
- correct connector identification,
- correct finite verb identification,
- correct dependency classification,
- or resolve genuine structural ambiguity.

Stage 4 review may NOT:

- override explicit ROOTS rules,
- retain subordinate clauses because they appear central,
- or replace mechanics with interpretive reasoning.

------

# 4. Stage 4 Audit Standards

## 4.1 Purpose of the Audit

The purpose of the Stage 4 audit is not merely to evaluate plausibility.

The audit exists to determine whether:

- the reviewer remained submitted to ROOTS constraints,
- and whether the output remains mechanically reproducible.

------

# 5. Stage 4 Audit Categories

## 5.1 Explicit ROOTS Violations

Definition:

A review output contradicts an explicit ROOTS rule.

Examples:

- subordinate clause retained as trunk,
- non-finite verb treated as clause anchor,
- connector dependency ignored,
- reviewer reasoning overrides mechanics.

These are considered hard violations.

------

## 5.2 Interpretive Retention Drift

Audit for review language involving:

- governing force,
- central idea,
- rhetorical importance,
- dominant movement,
- thematic prominence,
- main point,
- exhortational centrality,
- discourse weighting.

These categories indicate interpretive drift entering the review layer.

------

## 5.3 Confidence Contamination

Audit elevated confidence values paired with:

- interpretive reasoning,
- rhetorical prioritization,
- or reviewer persuasion.

Confidence must remain tied to mechanical certainty.

------

## 5.4 Mechanical Inconsistency

Audit for:

- inconsistent connector handling,
- inconsistent dependency treatment,
- inconsistent clause retention,
- or inconsistent structural application.

Mechanical inconsistency destroys reproducibility.

------

# 6. Stage 4 Correction Philosophy

Stage 4 corrections must:

- restore submission to ROOTS constraints,
- restore mechanical consistency,
- restore reproducibility,
- and remove interpretive override.

Stage 4 corrections must NOT optimize:

- elegance,
- readability,
- rhetorical quality,
- theological coherence,
- or persuasive force.

The goal is methodological fidelity.

------

# 7. Stage 4 Implementation Requirements

## 7.1 Constraint Enforcement Layer

Stage 4 review must implement an explicit constraint enforcement layer before review decisions are accepted.

This layer must evaluate review outputs against ROOTS governing constraints.

The reviewer may not bypass this layer.

------

## 7.2 Required Automatic Audit Flags

The following audit flags must exist explicitly in the Stage 4 review system.

### SUBORDINATE_CONNECTOR_RETAINED

Trigger:

- a clause introduced by a subordinating connector is retained in trunk.

Examples:

- ἵνα
- ὅτι
- ἐάν
- εἰ
- καθώς
- ὡς

Result:

- automatic review failure,
- or mandatory explicit override justification category.

------

### NON_FINITE_VERB_AS_ANCHOR

Trigger:

- participle,
- infinitive,
- or non-finite verbal form treated as a finite clause anchor.

Result:

- automatic review failure.

------

### INTERPRETIVE_RETENTION_LANGUAGE

Trigger review notes containing concepts such as:

- governing force,
- rhetorical prominence,
- thematic centrality,
- main point,
- dominant movement,
- discourse peak,
- exhortational importance.

Result:

- audit warning,
- confidence reduction,
- or mandatory re-review.

------

### CONFIDENCE_EXCEEDS_MECHANICAL_CERTAINTY

Trigger:

- HIGH or MEDIUM-HIGH confidence paired with:
  - interpretive language,
  - rhetorical weighting,
  - or unresolved mechanical conflict.

Result:

- audit warning,
- mandatory review,
- or confidence downgrade.

------

### RULE_OVERRIDE_DETECTED

Trigger:

- reviewer output contradicts explicit ROOTS mechanics.

Examples:

- subordinate dependency ignored,
- connector classification overridden,
- finite verb rule bypassed.

Result:

- automatic review failure.

------

## 7.3 Stage 4 Review Philosophy

Stage 4 review exists to:

- verify mechanics,
- resolve structural ambiguity,
- and preserve reproducibility.

Stage 4 review does NOT exist to:

- optimize rhetoric,
- preserve persuasive flow,
- improve theological coherence,
- or protect perceived centrality.

------

## 7.4 Correction Procedure

When a violation is identified:

1. The explicit ROOTS rule must be identified.
2. The violation category must be identified.
3. The trunk must be corrected mechanically.
4. Confidence must be reassessed.
5. Review notes must avoid interpretive retention language.

------

## 7.5 Future Automation Goal

Future Stage 4 tooling should automatically:

- detect subordinating connector retention,
- detect finite/non-finite violations,
- detect interpretive review language,
- detect inconsistent connector handling,
- and detect confidence contamination.

The goal is increasing mechanical reproducibility across all future books.

------

# 8. Stage 4 Implementation Plan

## 8.1 Immediate Implementation Goal

The immediate goal is not to redesign Stage 4.

The goal is to constrain the existing Stage 4 review layer so that ROOTS mechanics cannot be silently overridden.

------

## 8.2 Required Stage 4 Refactor

The current Stage 4 flow:

```text
review
→ confidence
→ export
```

must become:

```text
review
→ ROOTS constraint audit
→ violation flags
→ confidence validation
→ export
```

The audit layer must execute BEFORE export.

------

## 8.3 Required Audit Inputs

The Stage 4 audit layer must inspect:

- connector classification,
- dependency classification,
- finite verb status,
- trunk retention,
- confidence value,
- and review note language.

------

## 8.4 Required Connector Enforcement

The audit layer must explicitly inspect:

- ἵνα
- ὅτι
- ἐάν
- εἰ
- καθώς
- ὡς

If a subordinate connector is present and the clause is retained in trunk:

- automatic audit flag,
- automatic confidence downgrade,
- or automatic review failure.

No silent override is permitted.

------

## 8.5 Review Note Scanner

Stage 4 must implement a review-note scanner for interpretive drift language.

Examples:

- governing force
- central idea
- dominant movement
- rhetorical prominence
- exhortational center
- thematic weight
- discourse peak

These phrases indicate reviewer interpretation entering the review layer.

The scanner must:

- flag the row,
- reduce confidence,
- or require manual re-review.

------

## 8.6 Confidence Validation Layer

Confidence values must be validated against:

- mechanical certainty,
- explicit ROOTS rules,
- and audit violations.

Examples:

```text
subordinate clause retained
+ HIGH confidence
→ invalid confidence state
```

Confidence must not exceed methodological certainty.

------

## 8.7 Re-review Priority Order

Existing Stage 4 rows should be re-audited in this order:

1. subordinate connector retention
2. explicit rule overrides
3. interpretive review language
4. confidence contamination
5. inconsistent connector handling

------

## 8.8 Stage 4 Completion Standard

Stage 4 should not be considered complete merely because review exists.

Stage 4 is complete only when:

- ROOTS constraints are enforceable,
- audit violations are detectable,
- reviewer drift is constrained,
- confidence reflects mechanics,
- and rebuild outputs remain reproducible.

------

# 9. Governing Principle Summary

ROOTS is governed by explicit methodological constraints.

The reviewer is constrained by the methodology.

The methodology is not reshaped by reviewer instinct.

Mechanical reproducibility outranks interpretive sophistication.

This constraint system exists to preserve:

- objectivity,
- consistency,
- reproducibility,
- and structural integrity across all future books and review stages.

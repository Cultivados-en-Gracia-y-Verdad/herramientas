# ROOTS Audit Semantics and Classification Standard

## Purpose

This document formalizes the semantic meaning of ROOTS audit classifications.

The purpose of this document is to:

- preserve objectivity,
- prevent interpretive correction drift,
- distinguish proven violations from unresolved structure,
- and standardize audit reasoning across all future review layers.

This document governs:

- Stage 4 audit interpretation,
- future automated audits,
- confidence handling,
- reviewer response behavior,
- and violation classification.

------

# 1. ROOTS Audit Philosophy

ROOTS audit classifications must remain:

- mechanical,
- objective,
- reproducible,
- and structurally demonstrable.

Audit classifications may NOT:

- infer rhetorical importance,
- optimize readability,
- preserve argument elegance,
- or introduce interpretive weighting.

The audit system exists to measure methodological fidelity.

It does not exist to improve interpretation.

------

# 2. Fundamental Distinction

ROOTS must distinguish between:

1. proven violations,
2. unresolved mechanics,
3. and interpretive contamination.

Failure to distinguish these categories leads to:

- false positives,
- unstable review,
- interpretive correction drift,
- and loss of reproducibility.

------

# 3. FAIL Classification

## Definition

FAIL means:

A mechanically demonstrable ROOTS violation has occurred.

The violation must be structurally provable.

FAIL classifications may not be assigned from:

- intuition,
- plausibility,
- rhetorical dissatisfaction,
- or reviewer discomfort.

------

## Examples

### Proven subordinate clause retained

A subordinate clause is mechanically demonstrated to remain in trunk reduction contrary to ROOTS dependency rules.

------

### Non-finite verb treated as clause anchor

A participle or infinitive is treated as a finite clause anchor.

------

### Explicit ROOTS rule override

The review layer contradicts an explicit ROOTS mechanical rule.

------

## FAIL Requirements

A FAIL classification requires:

1. identifiable ROOTS rule,
2. mechanically demonstrable contradiction,
3. explicit structural evidence.

------

# 4. WARN Classification

## Definition

WARN means:

A structure is mechanically unresolved or insufficiently provable.

WARN does NOT mean:

- the structure is wrong,
- the review failed,
- or a violation has been demonstrated.

WARN indicates:

- review required,
- scope unresolved,
- or structural uncertainty.

------

## Examples

### Connector token present

A subordinating connector token appears in the trunk, but clause scope is not yet mechanically proven.

This does not automatically prove subordinate retention.

------

### Embedded dependency ambiguity

A dependency relationship may exist, but scope inheritance is unresolved mechanically.

------

### Structural incompleteness

The audit layer lacks sufficient structural resolution to classify the row as FAIL.

------

## WARN Requirements

WARN classifications must:

- identify the unresolved structure,
- explain the uncertainty mechanically,
- and avoid interpretive resolution.

WARN may NOT silently escalate into interpretive correction.

------

# 5. FLAG Classification

## Definition

FLAG means:

Interpretive contamination risk has been detected.

FLAG is methodological, not structural.

A FLAG does not necessarily mean:

- the trunk is incorrect,
- or the structure is invalid.

Instead, FLAG indicates:

- reviewer drift risk,
- interpretive language contamination,
- or confidence contamination.

------

## Examples

### Rhetorical weighting language

Review notes contain:

- governing force,
- rhetorical prominence,
- central point,
- thematic importance,
- dominant movement,
- discourse peak,
- or similar categories.

These categories exceed ROOTS mechanical constraints.

------

### Confidence contamination

Elevated confidence is assigned from:

- rhetorical persuasiveness,
- interpretive satisfaction,
- or thematic coherence,

rather than mechanical certainty.

------

## FLAG Requirements

FLAG classifications must:

- identify the contamination risk,
- distinguish it from structural failure,
- and avoid converting interpretive concern into mechanical judgment.

------

# 6. Audit Precision Principle

ROOTS audit precision must always prefer:

- mechanical demonstrability,
- explicit evidence,
- and reproducibility.

ROOTS must avoid:

- probabilistic interpretation,
- rhetorical optimization,
- or semantic intuition.

Where proof is insufficient:

- WARN is preferred over FAIL.

Where interpretive contamination is detected:

- FLAG is preferred over structural override.

------

# 7. Dependency Scope Principle

ROOTS removes subordinate clauses mechanically.

ROOTS does NOT remove:

- every connector token,
- every dependent-looking phrase,
- or every clause containing subordinating vocabulary.

Dependency removal requires:

- mechanically demonstrable subordinate scope.

Therefore:

connector presence alone is insufficient for FAIL classification.

------

# 8. Correction Constraint Principle

Audit findings may NOT be corrected through interpretation.

Corrections must proceed from:

- explicit mechanics,
- structural evidence,
- and ROOTS constraints.

The following are prohibited correction methods:

- rhetorical optimization,
- thematic balancing,
- argument preservation,
- interpretive smoothing,
- or theological harmonization.

------

# 9. Long-Term Audit Goal

The long-term goal of ROOTS auditing is:

- increasing mechanical precision,
- increasing reproducibility,
- reducing reviewer drift,
- and constraining interpretive contamination.

The audit system must mature toward:

- provable structure,
- explicit classification,
- and mechanically demonstrable reasoning.

------

# 10. Governing Summary

ROOTS auditing exists to preserve methodological fidelity.

The audit system must distinguish:

- demonstrable failure,
- unresolved structure,
- and interpretive contamination.

Mechanical proof outranks interpretive confidence.

Reviewer intuition may not override explicit ROOTS constraints.

Where proof is insufficient:

- WARN is preferred.

Where interpretive drift is detected:

- FLAG is preferred.

ROOTS auditing must remain:

- mechanical,
- objective,
- precise,
- reproducible,
- and structurally demonstrable.

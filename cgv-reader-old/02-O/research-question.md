# Research Question

## Can Greek finite-verb clauses be classified as dependent by objective grammatical criteria?

### Purpose

We are developing a Bible reading environment whose purpose is to help readers observe the grammatical structure of the Greek New Testament through objective, repeatable observations.

The project is intentionally conservative.

We are only interested in observations that can be demonstrated directly from the Greek text without requiring interpretive judgment.

Our goal is to help readers participate with the text, not to automate interpretation.

---

## Current State

The following observations have already been established objectively:

- finite verbs
- verbal mood
- imperative commands
- person and number
- clause spans (built by the reader)

The reader identifies the words belonging to each finite verb, producing complete finite-verb clauses.

At this point we have a list of completed clauses.

---

## The Remaining Question

Once finite-verb clauses have already been identified:

How can one determine objectively that a clause is grammatically dependent upon another clause?

We are not asking how to identify clauses.

That step has already been completed.

We are asking how Greek grammar establishes dependency between completed clauses.

---

## What We Are NOT Asking

We are not asking about:

- discourse analysis
- semantic relationships
- theological interpretation
- rhetorical analysis
- authorial intention

We are interested only in grammatical dependency.

---

## Our Current Observation

Many dependent clauses appear to begin with words such as:

- ἵνα
- εἰ
- ἐάν
- ὅταν

However, relative clauses introduce additional questions.

For example, clauses beginning with relative pronouns also appear dependent, but we do not wish to assume this merely because it seems obvious.

Our concern is objectivity rather than intuition.

---

## The Question

Is there an objective grammatical criterion by which Greek grammarians determine that one finite-verb clause depends upon another?

More specifically:

- Is dependency determined by identifiable grammatical markers?
- If so, what are those markers?
- If not, where does grammatical objectivity end and interpretation begin?

---

## Why We Are Asking

If such grammatical criteria exist, we would like to build observation tools that expose them directly to readers.

If such criteria do not exist, we do not wish to invent them.

Our objective is not to simplify Greek grammar beyond what the language itself permits.

Rather, we want to know precisely what can and cannot be demonstrated objectively from the text.

We would be grateful for any guidance, references, or published work that addresses this specific question.
